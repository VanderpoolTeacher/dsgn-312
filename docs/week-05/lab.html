Week 5 Lab — The covered-logo test

DSGN 312 · Packaging Design · in the room and just outside it

About 50 minutes. Not graded. What you capture here is the evidence for this week's assignment, which is a module summative.

Terms follow the Glossary.

What you need

  • A package from the front. Not your own. A familiar brand tests your memory, not the design.
  • The audit sheet.
  • Masking material — opaque, not translucent.
  • Access to strangers. They have been arranged.

Steps

1 · Take a package you do not recognise. If you know the brand well, swap it.

2 · Find every mark. Front, back, base, seal, closure, embossing, and inside the legal text. You saw the demo — expect five or six, not two.

3 · Mask them all. Opaque material, pressed down. A lifted corner shows the mark at exactly the angle somebody tilts a package to read it.

4 · Have a classmate check your masking before you go. Sixty seconds. They will find one you missed. This is not optional — one visible mark invalidates everything after it.

5 · Write your prediction. One sentence: what will they say it is, who is it for, what does it cost? Before you leave the room.

6 · Find a stranger. Not a classmate who watched you mask it. Not a friend who knows the brand.

7 · Ask three questions, in this order:

  • What do you think this is?
  • Who is it for?
  • What would you guess it costs?

8 · Write the answers word for word. Not summarised. The exact words are the evidence — a paraphrase quietly moves them toward what you expected to hear.

9 · Say nothing while they think. Do not help, do not prompt, do not explain. The silence is uncomfortable and it is the test.

10 · Compare against your prediction, in writing, before you come back.

11 · If they matched exactly — find a second stranger. Agreement of one is a coincidence you have not tested. This step is the difference between a finding and an anecdote.

What you are looking for

The gap. Where what you expected and what they said diverge, and which design decision produced the difference.

What you predicted What they said The gap is telling you
"Premium skincare, women, £30" "Some kind of medicine? Chemist thing. Eight quid." The clinical white and the sans-serif read as pharmacy, not luxury
"Kids' snack, cheap" "Looks organic. Expensive-ish." Uncoated board is doing more than the illustration
"No idea what they'll say" "Washing powder. No idea who for. Cheap." Category convention, and nothing else. This is a real finding

If your package has no identity at all

Some own-brand packaging carries nothing beyond category convention. The stranger says "washing powder, no idea, cheap" and is completely right.

This is a legitimate result and it is harder to write up than a strong one. Your audit then documents an absence: what the package could have carried and does not. Do not swap it for something easier.

Before you leave

  • Verbatim answers from at least one stranger — two if the first matched your prediction.
  • Your written prediction, dated before the test.
  • The masking checked by somebody else.
  • One sentence naming the design decision that explains the biggest gap.
