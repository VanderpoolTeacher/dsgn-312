Week 5 Assignment — The shelf study

DSGN 312 · Packaging Design

Formative · satisfactory/unsatisfactory · required. Competency 1, Competency Rubric.

What to hand in

  1. The shelf photograph, whole row
  2. Two competitors, diagrammed at both distances — four diagrams
  3. One contrast sentence per package, explaining the first read
  4. Your partner's independent numbering, and where it differed from yours
  5. One sentence per package on which distance it was designed for

About a page, plus images.

You're done when

Your explanation is mechanical — it names what the contrast is against.

"It stands out because it's bolder" restates the observation.

"It's the only matte surface in a row of gloss, so it holds still while the others glare" explains it.

The test: does your sentence name the other term? If the contrast could be true of the package sitting alone on a white background, you have described it rather than explained it.

Satisfactory

  • Four diagrams, both distances, numbered on the image
  • Two contrast sentences, each naming what the contrast is against
  • The partner's numbering reproduced, including disagreement

Not yet

  • Taste language — cleaner, nicer, more premium, eye-catching — with no other term named
  • Diagrams at one distance only
  • Partner disagreement quietly resolved into agreement

Watch out for

Taste words. Cleaner, nicer, premium, striking, eye-catching. All are conclusions with the reasoning removed. The question that fixes every one of them: against what?

Explaining by audience. "It stands out because it's aimed at younger buyers." That is week 6's move and it is not available yet. The discipline this week is explaining by contrast alone, which is harder and more precise. Hold the line — it is the reason the two weeks are separate.

Resolving the disagreement. If your partner numbered it differently, report it. Talking each other into a shared answer destroys the only independent evidence in the exercise.

Analysing a product page. E-commerce has no shelf, no row, no neighbours, and therefore no contrast to name. Use the curated photographs instead.

Comparing packages that are not neighbours. Two products from different aisles are not competitors in the sense that matters. Contrast is against what is physically adjacent.

Why this is required

Week 6 is a four-lens critique and visual communication is one of the four. A student who has not diagrammed a hierarchy cannot evaluate one under time pressure with three other lenses to cover.

Missing this week produces a predictable, specific shortfall: a three-lens critique in a four-lens assessment. That is worth knowing in advance rather than discovering in the mark.
