AI as a design tool, and where it fails

DSGN 312 · Week 8 · Read this before the session — about 30 minutes

Terms follow the Glossary.

What this week is actually assessing

Read the outcome carefully:

Use AI-assisted tools to generate or visualize packaging concepts, while critically evaluating their outputs.

The whole outcome lives in the second half of that sentence.

Generating an image requires no judgment. A folder of striking concepts proves you have access to a tool. What is being assessed is whether you can look at an impressive output and say, specifically, what is wrong with it — in a way somebody else can verify.

Prompts that specify structure

Most people write style prompts. "Sustainable packaging for a candle, beautiful, minimal, earthy."

You will get something attractive and structurally meaningless — because nothing in that sentence describes an object that can be made.

A structural prompt names things a manufacturer would need:

  • A form — straight tuck box, sleeve over tray, tray and lid
  • A material with a weight — 350 gsm uncoated board
  • A closure — tuck flap, magnetic, friction fit
  • Panel count and proportion
  • What is printed on which panel

Compare:

"Eco-friendly packaging for a soap bar, natural, artisanal"

"A straight tuck box for a 90×60×25 mm soap bar, 350 gsm uncoated kraft, tuck flap top and bottom, one glue tab, product name on the front panel, ingredients on the back"

The second gets you something you can argue with. That is the point — an output you can argue with is an output you can evaluate.

What these tools are reliably wrong about

You are given the categories because the week is short. Look for these:

Structures that cannot fold flat

The most common failure. Generated packaging routinely shows forms that could not be cut from one sheet — panels that would need to occupy the same space, folds going in impossible directions, a box that is a rendering of a box rather than a foldable object.

Flaps and tabs with nowhere to go

A tuck flap drawn with no slot to tuck into. A glue tab on a face that is covered at that point in assembly. These look completely convincing.

Substrates that do not exist at that thickness

Board rendered as thin as paper and rigid as plastic. Material behaviour is where these tools are weakest, because behaviour is not visible in the training images.

Finishes that cannot go on that material

Spot UV on uncoated board. Foil on a compostable film. Both are shown constantly and neither is manufacturable.

Claims that could not legally appear

Generated packaging invents certification marks, recycling symbols, and health claims. It will confidently produce a recycling logo that does not exist.

Continuous artwork across an impossible surface

Imagery that flows unbroken around a form that has folds — meaning the artwork ignores the creases the object would actually have.

What counts as a checkable failure

Somebody else has to be able to confirm it from the image.

Not checkable Checkable
"It didn't understand structure" "It drew a tuck flap with no slot to tuck into"
"The material looks wrong" "It shows spot UV on uncoated kraft — that finish cannot go on that stock"
"The proportions are off" "The front panel is 2:1 and the side panel is drawn the same width, so the box is impossible"
"It's generic" "It invented a recycling mark — that symbol does not exist"

Test: hand your claim and the image to a classmate. Can they confirm it? If they cannot, you have an impression.

Attribution: whose fault was it?

This is 8.3 and it is the hardest idea in the week.

When an output fails, there are two possibilities:

The prompt caused it. You never specified a structure, so the tool invented one. That is your failure, and the fix is a better prompt.

The tool caused it. You specified a straight tuck box with a glue tab and it still drew something unfoldable. That is a limit of the tool, and the fix is to stop asking it for that.

The distinction decides what you do next, and it is only recoverable if you kept the prompt. This is why the log is the deliverable and the images are supporting material.

Two ways to fail this week

The enthusiast. Finds the outputs brilliant, submits a folder of beautiful images, evaluates nothing. "It helped me brainstorm."

The sceptic. Declares the outputs worthless, submits a short dismissal, evaluates nothing.

Both score the same, and both score badly, because neither has done the thing the outcome names. Uncritical rejection is not criticism.

Vocabulary this week owns

Term What it means here

Checkable failure Something in a generated concept that could not be made, which another person can confirm from the image
Attribution Deciding whether a failure came from an underspecified prompt or from the tool

Self-check

  1. Why does the outcome live in the second half of its own sentence? (What this week is assessing)
  2. Rewrite a style prompt as a structural one. What did you have to add? (Prompts that specify structure)
  3. Name four things these tools are reliably wrong about. (What these tools are reliably wrong about)
  4. Turn "the material looks wrong" into a checkable failure. (What counts as checkable)
  5. Your prompt did not name a structure and the output is unfoldable. Whose failure? (Attribution)
  6. Why do the enthusiast and the sceptic score the same? (Two ways to fail)

Bring to the session

  • Three prompts for your own concept, unrun.
