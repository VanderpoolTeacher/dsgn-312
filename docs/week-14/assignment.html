Week 14 Assignment — Draft case study

DSGN 312 · Packaging Design

Formative · satisfactory/unsatisfactory · required. Competency 5, Competency Rubric.

What to hand in

A draft case study, in this order:

  1. The problem — product, audience, constraint
  2. The outcome — what you made, photographed properly
  3. The route — three dated states, with what caused each change
  4. What is still wrong — the weakest remaining point in your claim

Plus your production files, ready for week 15.

You're done when

Three dated states, and each change names what caused it.

Satisfactory

  • Three states, dated, genuinely from different points in the term
  • Each transition names an antecedent — a test result, a critique, a material or print constraint
  • The weakest point named plainly
  • Photographs legible at projection size

Not yet

  • States that differ only cosmetically — a colourway, a type swap. That is one design shown three times
  • Dates showing all three produced together after the design settled
  • Changes described as improvements with nothing causing them
  • "No weaknesses"

Watch out for

Manufacturing the early states. The dates give it away, and the rubric names it in the Not yet row. Two honest states beat three where one is invented.

Chronology. First, then, then, then. Problem and outcome first.

Vague causes. "Feedback suggested…" Which feedback? From whom? Doing what? Your week 11 record has the specifics — use them.

Claiming no weakness. Everyone's claim has one. Not finding it means not looking, and week 15 will ask.

Phone-sized photographs. Check them on a screen.

If you have two states

Say so. Do not manufacture a third.

A student with two honest states and a clear account of why the third does not exist is in a better position than one whose dates contradict their story — and the dates are visible.

Why the weakest point is worth writing now

Week 15's rubric awards Exceeds for volunteering the weakest point in your sustainability claim before being asked.

You are being told this in advance because knowing it does not make it easy. The students who do it well are the ones who wrote the sentence a week earlier, when they were not standing in front of a room.

Submission

Draft case study before the final session. Bring the mockups to week 15 — the presentation cannot be scored as intended without the object.
