Week 1 Lab — Unboxing critique

DSGN 312 · Packaging Design · in the room, in pairs

About 45 minutes. Not graded. What you produce here becomes the raw material for this week's assignment, which is.

Terms follow the Glossary.

What you need

  • Your package, still closed. If you opened it at home, take a spare from the front.
  • The record sheet, printed or on screen.
  • Your written prediction from the lesson's self-check 4.
  • A partner.

Cutting tools are available. Ask before using one — some packages are better opened badly, because that is the finding.

The rules

1 · Nobody opens anything before we start. An already-opened package has lost its evidence. This is the one instruction that cannot be recovered from.

2 · The opener narrates. The recorder writes. You will swap.

3 · The recorder writes what happened, not what it means.

✅ "Tried the tab, it did not lift, switched to tearing the side."

❌ "The tab was badly designed."

The second is a conclusion. You will draw conclusions afterwards, and you can only draw good ones from an accurate record.

Steps

  1. Read your prediction aloud to your partner. Where do you think it will resist?

  2. Open it slowly, narrating. Say what you are trying, in the order you try it. Where your hands go. What you expected to happen. Slower than feels natural — the useful moments are the half-seconds where you hesitated.

  3. Your partner records the sequence. Actions and outcomes only.

  4. Stop when it is open. Do not tidy up, do not flatten it.

  5. Swap and repeat.

  6. Choose three friction points from your own opening. Write one sentence each, locating the fault in the object.

  7. Point at every one. The package stays on the desk and you put a finger on the thing you named. This is the check. If you cannot point at it, you have described a feeling — rewrite it or choose a different point.

  8. Compare against your prediction. Where were you wrong? Being wrong is the more interesting result and it is worth a sentence.

  9. Round the room — one friction point each, no repeats. After the first six or seven the obvious ones are gone and you will have to look harder. That is the intended difficulty.

What counts, and what does not

Counts Does not
"The die-cut stops 2 mm short of the fold, so the tab lifts but never releases." "The tab is fiddly."
"The film seal has no start point — there is nowhere to get a fingernail in." "Impossible to open."
"Both flaps are glued; opening it destroys the panel with the reorder code." "They over-engineered it."
"The perforation runs across the grain, so it tears into the printed face." "Cheap material."

The left column can be checked by somebody else holding the same object. That is the only difference, and it is the whole standard.

If your package turns out to be well made

This happens and it is not a problem with you. Some packaging is genuinely good.

Ask for one of the awkward items held at the front. There are two or three deliberately difficult objects for exactly this. A package with no friction gives you nothing to record, and you need three.

Before you leave

  • Three friction points, written, each one you have physically pointed at.
  • One sentence on where your prediction was wrong.
  • Your package — keep it. You may want it in week 2.
