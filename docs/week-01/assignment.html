Week 1 Assignment — The unboxing record

DSGN 312 · Packaging Design

Formative · satisfactory/unsatisfactory · required.

Assessed against Competency 4 of the Competency Rubric, at an introductory level. Terms follow the Glossary.

What to hand in

One document. One package. Three points of friction.

For each point:

  1. What happened, in the order it happened.
  2. Where the fault is in the object — the specific physical feature.
  3. A photograph with the feature visible.

Then, at the end:

  1. One sentence on where your prediction was wrong, from the lesson's self-check 4.

That is the whole assignment. It should run to about a page.

You're done when

Somebody else, handed your package, would find what you found.

That is the entire standard. Read each of your three points and ask it. If the answer is "only if they felt the same way I did," it is not there yet.

The check, concretely

Your sentence Would another person find it?
"It was hard to open." No — that is your experience
"The lid was stiff." No — compared to what?
"The lid has no lip, so there is nothing to hook a thumb under." Yes — they can look and confirm

Watch out for

Describing your reaction instead of the object. This is the failure the assignment exists to catch, and almost everybody does it at least once. Annoying, fiddly, frustrating, cheap, over-engineered — all of them are about you.

Blaming the manufacturer. "They cut corners" is a theory about a company. Stay on the object; week 12 is where judgment about the maker becomes legitimate, and it has a rubric behind it by then.

Three points that are really one point. A tab that will not lift, a tab that tears, and a tab that leaves residue are three observations about one feature. Find friction in three different places.

Photographs where the feature is not visible. A photograph of the whole package does not show a die-cut stopping 2 mm short. Get close.

Why this is required

It is worth nothing toward your grade and you cannot skip it.

Everything this course assesses later depends on the habit it builds: locating a fault in an object rather than in your reaction to it. Week 6 asks you to do it across four lenses. Week 12 asks you to do it live, on a package you have never seen, and that session is the only evidence for one of the six things this course claims to teach.

This is the cheapest possible place to find out you describe feelings instead of objects. It costs a page.

Submission

One document, with photographs, submitted before the next session.

Keep your package. Week 2 may use it.
