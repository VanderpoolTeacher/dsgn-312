Week 6 Assignment — Persona and moodboard

DSGN 312 · Packaging Design

Module summative, together with the critique delivered in the lab. Competencies 1 and 4, Competency Rubric.

What to hand in

  1. An audience statement, one sentence, that rules something out
  2. Two design decisions it forces, named
  3. A moodboard — visual references for that audience, annotated with what each reference is doing
  4. One paragraph on your critique: which lens was strongest, which was weakest, and whether you predicted it correctly

About a page, plus the moodboard.

You're done when

Your audience statement forces two design decisions, and you can name them.

That is the test, and it is binary. Either a decision is forced or it is not.

Meets

  • The statement rules something out
  • Two decisions named, each traceable to the statement
  • The moodboard is annotated — each reference says what it is doing, not just that it is there
  • The critique reflection names a specific lens, not a general impression

Not yet

  • A demographic bracket — "young professionals," "eco-conscious families"
  • Decisions that would be true of any audience
  • An unannotated moodboard
  • "I think it went okay"

Watch out for

The bracket. Eco-conscious millennials, busy parents, design-savvy urbanites. All of them describe a market segment and none of them decides anything. Ask what your statement rules out. If the answer is nothing, rewrite it.

Decisions that are not forced. "So the packaging should be high quality." Every audience wants that. A forced decision is one that would be wrong for a different audience — a re-sealable closure, an unreadable price, a one-handed opening.

A moodboard as a mood. Twelve attractive images prove taste. Annotate each one: what is this reference doing that serves the audience?

A vague critique reflection. "I could have said more." Name the lens. You wrote a prediction before class — check it. If you predicted accessibility and it was accessibility, that is a real and useful finding about yourself, eleven weeks before it costs you anything.

Why the reflection matters more than it looks

Week 12 is this critique with the preparation removed, and it is the only evidence for LO 4 in the entire course.

The lens you were thin on this week is the lens you will be thin on then — it is stable. Naming it now is most of the fix, and you have six weeks.

Students who leave this week knowing exactly which lens they drop are the ones who do not drop it in week 12.

Before the next session. The critique itself was assessed in the room.
