4 / 14

"Someone buying a gift they will not see opened"

  • The interior has to look finished
  • The closure has to be re-sealable
  • The price has to be unreadable at a glance

Three decisions. One sentence.

5 / 14

The test

Name two decisions it forces

If you cannot, it is a bracket, not an audience.

6 / 14

Four lenses

  • Market relevance — right shelf, right price, against these neighbours?
  • Ethics — does it claim what it does not deliver?
  • Accessibility — can the person who most needs it get at it?
  • Visual communication — does the first read deliver?

Each finds what the others cannot.

7 / 14

Accessibility is not "large type"

  • Dosage reversed out of a mid-tone at 6 pt
  • A seal needing two hands and opposing force
  • Variants distinguished only by colour
  • A closure needing a pincer grip
  • Information across a fold

Fault → change

Something has to happen next.

9 / 14

The pattern

  1. Name the fault — in the object
  2. Say what it costs — the reader, the buyer, the claim
  3. Say what would change

10 / 14

You don't have to be able to fix everything

"This has to change and I don't see a cheap way" — a critique.

"It's fine" — not.

11 / 14

Lab — four lenses, aloud

  • 5 minutes alone with the object
  • 2 minutes with a neighbour
  • ~3 minutes delivering
  • The room asks: "and so what should change?"
  • Second pass if a lens found nothing
