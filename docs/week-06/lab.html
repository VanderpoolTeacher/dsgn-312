Week 6 Lab — The four-lens critique

DSGN 312 · Packaging Design · in the room, aloud

About 55 minutes. This one is assessed — it is part of the module summative, because a critique is a performance and there is nothing to submit.

That is the same shape as week 12, deliberately.

What you need

  • The four-lens sheet
  • A package — handed to you in the room
  • Your predicted weakest lens, from the lesson's self-check

How it runs

1 · You are handed a package you did not bring.

2 · Five minutes alone with it. Notes allowed.

Week 12 gives you four minutes and nothing else on this list.

3 · Two minutes conferring with a neighbour.

Week 12 removes this entirely.

4 · Deliver the critique aloud — about three minutes. Four lenses, any order.

5 · The room asks "and so what should change?" whenever a fault arrives without one.

Peers ask it, not only the instructor. Being asked by a classmate is what makes the habit stick.

6 · After each critique, the room names any lens that found nothing. Countable, immediate, unarguable.

7 · If you were short, you go again on the same package, immediately.

Week 12 has no second pass. This is the most valuable minute of the module and it is not skipped for time.

Working the four lenses under time

Five minutes is not long. A workable order:

Order Lens Look for
1 Accessibility Do it first — it is the one you will forget. Grip, one-handed opening, type size on required text, colour-only distinctions
2 Ethics Read every claim. Which are checkable, which name a feeling?
3 Visual communication Number the first read. Does the order deliver?
4 Market relevance Where is this, at what price, against whom?

Do accessibility first. It is last on nobody's list by accident — it is unfamiliar, so it goes when time runs out.

What a delivered fault sounds like

❌ "The back panel is cluttered."

✅ "The back panel runs the ingredients, the recycling mark and the barcode into one block with no spacing, so the allergen line has nothing separating it — someone scanning for it has to read the whole thing. It needs a rule above it and a line of space."

Fault. What it costs. What changes.

If a lens genuinely finds nothing

Say so, and say what you looked for.

"On ethics — it makes no sustainability claim at all, and there's no percentage or certificate to check. So there's nothing to challenge, and that's a choice worth noticing in this category."

That is a covered lens. Silence is not.

Before you leave

  • Four lenses delivered, each with something the others did not find
  • Every fault carrying a change
  • Your predicted weakest lens against your actual weakest — were you right?
