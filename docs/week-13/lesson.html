Production

DSGN 312 · Week 13 · Read this before the session — about 30 minutes

Terms follow the Glossary.

Settings have no correct value in the abstract

There is no right resolution. There is no right colour space. There is no right amount of bleed.

There is only what a named process requires.

This is why the first thing you write down is the destination: who would print this, on what press or process, on what substrate, at what quantity. Every subsequent decision answers to that line, and without it you are choosing defaults and hoping.

"Digital press, short run of 200, on my specified 350 gsm uncoated board, single-sided, cut and creased on a flatbed cutter."

Now bleed, colour space, resolution and finish all have someone to answer to.

The layers, finalised

The convention was set in week 9. This is where it is enforced, because a printer reads these literally.

Layer Holds Consequence if wrong
CUT Every line the blade goes through A crease here becomes a cut. The package falls apart on the line
CREASE Every line the board folds along A cut here does not fold — it is scored through
ARTWORK The surface design —
DIELINE-REF Your dimensions, labels and notes Left in the export, it prints

Files are inspected in the clinic, not described. A student who says the layers are separated and has one stray crease on the cut layer is not lying — they have not looked.

Bleed, through folds, for the third time

Week 9 introduced it, the demo showed the white sliver, and it is still the commonest error in submitted files.

Artwork that meets a fold runs at least 3 mm past it into the adjoining panel. The fold is never exactly where the file says, and the board's own thickness pulls the surface around the corner.

Check it on every fold, against your physical mockup.

Resolution and colour space, decided rather than defaulted

Resolution. 300 ppi at final size is the usual answer for litho or digital print — but the question is at final size, and students routinely place an image that was 300 ppi before they scaled it up by 150%.

Colour space. CMYK for most print. Which profile depends on the process and the substrate — uncoated board absorbs ink and prints duller and darker than coated, and an uncoated profile compensates. Using a coated profile on uncoated stock produces a result that is muddier than the proof.

This is where week 4 comes back. You chose a substrate for structural and disposal reasons. It also determines how your colour behaves, and if you have not accounted for that, the printed result will not match what is on your screen.

Finishes, and what they cannot go on

Spot varnish, emboss, deboss, foil, soft-touch lamination — each has substrates it cannot be applied to.

The common collision in this course: a student specifies an uncoated, recyclable, unlaminated board in week 4 for good reasons, then wants spot UV on it in week 13. The finish cannot go there, and one of the two has to give.

The substrate stays. It was reasoned, it carries LO 3, and it is part of your material claim. The finish was decoration.

Better to find this now than never — and it is a genuinely good sentence in week 15's presentation: "I wanted a spot varnish and my own material choice ruled it out."

What a handoff actually is

Files a printer could run without phoning you.

The test is not whether they are correct. It is whether they are interpretable by somebody who was not in the room. In the lab a partner will open yours cold — anything they cannot work out without you is not a handoff.

A handoff usually includes:

  • The dieline, layers named and separated
  • The artwork, correctly positioned, with bleed
  • An export to the destination's specification
  • A note stating substrate, finish, and quantity

Saying which requirement each setting satisfies

This is 13.3 and it is what the summative marks.

❌ "Exported as PDF/X-1a."

✅ "PDF/X-1a because the printer's sheet asks for it — it flattens transparency, which their RIP handles unpredictably."

❌ "3 mm bleed."

✅ "3 mm bleed because the trim drifts by up to a millimetre and artwork stopping at the edge leaves a white sliver on the fold."

A correct file with no reasoning behind it meets the technical standard and not the outcome.

Self-check

  1. Write your destination in one line. Who, on what, how many? (Settings have no correct value)
  2. What happens if a crease ends up on the cut layer? (The layers)
  3. Your image was 300 ppi and you scaled it 150%. What is it now? (Resolution)
  4. Why does an uncoated substrate change your colour decisions? (Colour space)
  5. You want spot UV on uncoated recyclable board. What gives, and why? (Finishes)
  6. Justify three of your own settings, each naming the requirement it answers. (Saying which requirement)

Bring to the session

  • Revised files, week 11's changes made
  • The dated pre-revision state, kept separately
  • Your destination, in one line
  • Your list of settings you cannot justify
