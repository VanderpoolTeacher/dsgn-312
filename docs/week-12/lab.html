Week 12 Lab — The critique performance

DSGN 312 · Packaging Design · in the room, aloud

This is the assessment. Not a rehearsal for one. It is scored as you speak, it produces no artifact, and it is the only evidence for LO 4 in this course.

What you need

Nothing. No notes, no reference material, no phone.

The package is supplied. That is the point.

How it runs

1 · Packages are distributed face down and stay untouched until your turn. Nobody browses the stock.

2 · Four minutes alone with your object. You may make notes now, on paper provided. You may not confer, look anything up, or use a phone.

Week 6 gave you five minutes, two minutes with a neighbour, and a second attempt. None of those exist today.

3 · Deliver the critique aloud — about four minutes. Four lenses, any order.

4 · Two questions from the instructor. These are part of the assessment and they will probe your thinnest lens.

5 · The room does not coach. Week 6 was collaborative. This is not.

What is being scored

Coverage Four lenses. Did each find something the others did not? Countable
Consequence Did every fault named carry what would have to change?
The questions Held reasoning, or recited preparation?

Coverage is countable and unarguable. A student who covers three lenses gets three-quarters of the coverage criterion however good the three were.

Use your four minutes in this order

1 · Accessibility. First. Always first. It is what you will run out of time for.

  • Pick it up. Open it. Can you, one-handed?
  • Find the required text. Read it at arm's length
  • Two variants? Is colour the only difference?
  • Anything printed across a fold or a curve?

2 · Ethics. Read every claim on the pack. Which name a number, a stream or a certificate? Which name a feeling?

3 · Visual communication. Number the first read. Does the order deliver what it should?

4 · Market relevance. Where does this sit, at what price, against whom?

Write one fault and one change per lens. Four and four. That is your critique.

Delivering it

Say the lens, then the fault, then the change.

"On accessibility — the dosage is 6 pt reversed out of a mid-tone. The person who most needs it is unwell and may be reading it at night. It moves to the flat panel at 10 pt minimum and loses the reverse."

Four of those, one per lens, is a complete critique in about four minutes.

If a lens genuinely finds nothing

Say so, and say what you looked for.

"On ethics — there's no sustainability claim at all, no percentage and no certificate. So there's nothing to challenge, and in this category that's a choice worth noticing."

That is a covered lens. Silence is not.

If you cannot fix it

Say that.

"This needs a different closure and I don't think there's a cheap way to get one — you'd be re-tooling. If it were mine I'd be arguing about whether the window survives."

A critique with an honest limit is a critique. Silence, or "it's fine," is not.

Absence

There is no artifact and no re-mark. If you cannot be here, tell the instructor beforehand — a make-up exists, on a different package you have not seen, and it has to be arranged rather than improvised.

A student who simply misses this session has no evidence for LO 4 anywhere in the course.
