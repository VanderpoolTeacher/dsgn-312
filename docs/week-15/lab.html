Week 15 — The presentations

DSGN 312 · Packaging Design · in the room

This is the assessment. Scored on the presentation rubric, independently of the work itself.

What you need

  • The physical mockups. All of them, product included
  • The production files, openable
  • The case study, with three dated states on screen

How it runs

1 · Mockups go on the tables before anyone presents. The room handles them during and after.

2 · About eight minutes each:

  • The problem — product, audience, and what the audience forced
  • What you specified and why — including the alternative that lost
  • Where the design changed and what caused it — three dated states, shown
  • How the sustainability claim holds, including where it does not

3 · Production files opened live, briefly. Name which requirement two or three settings answer.

4 · About five minutes of questions, instructor and room, object in hand.

5 · The three standing questions, plus one drawn from your own project.

What is being scored

The iteration account Three dated states. Does each revision name its cause and what would have gone wrong had it stayed?
The material defence Why this substrate beat the rejected one. Does the claim survive being pushed on?
The AI account A specific failure, what caused it, what you did instead
Production handoff Which requirement each setting answers

Plus, throughout: the questioning. Held reasoning or recited preparation.

Presenting with objects in the room

Let people pick things up. Do not talk over the handling — pause, let them open it, and watch. It is the last test your package gets and you will learn something from it.

Do not apologise for the object. "It's a bit rough" is the most common opening sentence in a final presentation and it costs you nothing but gains you nothing either. Say what it is and what it does.

Answering the questions

Take a second before answering. A pause reads as thought. An immediate answer to a hard question reads as a prepared one, which is precisely what the questioning is designed to detect.

If you do not know, say so and say what you would do to find out. That is a better answer than a confident guess, and it is scored better.

If you are asked something you already covered, do not just repeat it — say it a different way. Repeating verbatim is the clearest sign of a recited account.

After the last presentation

Ten minutes on what went wrong across the whole room this term. Most cohorts find the same three:

  • Closures that were obvious to their designer and to nobody else
  • Sustainability claims that thinned under a single question
  • AI concepts that were structurally impossible

Recognising the pattern is the last teaching of the course — these are not your personal failures, they are what packaging design does to everybody.
