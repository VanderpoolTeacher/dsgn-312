Week 15 Assignment — Phase 8, the final submission

DSGN 312 · Packaging Design

Module summative. Phase 8 — completes Component G.

Scored on the presentation rubric, independently of the summative rubric.

Competency 5, Competency Rubric.

What to hand in

  1. The case study — problem, outcome, route, what is still wrong. Three dated states.
  2. Production files, exported to your stated destination
  3. The presentation, delivered in the room with the mockups handled

You're done when

Each revision names the thing it responded to, and a printer could run your files without phoning you.

Meets

  • Three dated states shown, each revision naming its cause and what would have gone wrong
  • The material choice defended against the rejected alternative, and the claim survives being pushed on
  • A specific AI failure named, with what caused it and what you did instead
  • Production files shown, with settings explained against requirements

Exceeds

  • A revision you made and partly reversed, and what the reversal taught
  • The weakest point in your claim, volunteered before being asked
  • A setting that had to change for your substrate specifically

Not yet

  • States differing cosmetically, or dated together after the design settled
  • "It's fine" / "I'd polish it"
  • Reasoning that survives the deck and not the question

Watch out for

Re-presenting the object. It was scored in week 10. The account is what is being scored now. Time spent describing what the room can see in their hands is time not spent on why.

"I'd polish it." The most common answer to the two-weeks question and the weakest. Name the thing you know is still wrong.

Apologising for the mockup. Costs nothing, gains nothing.

Repeating verbatim under questioning. The clearest sign of a recited account. Say it a different way.

Leaving the mockups at home. The presentation cannot be scored as intended without them, and there is no second sitting.

Why the two rubrics are not reconciled

You may score high here and lower on the work, or the reverse.

Both are meaningful results, not errors. Making and accounting for the work are different competencies, and averaging them would let a polished presentation carry thin artifacts — or let a strong maker who cannot explain their decisions pass an outcome they have not met.

Submission

Case study and production files on the day. The presentation is delivered in the room.
