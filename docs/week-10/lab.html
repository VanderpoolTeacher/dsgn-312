Week 10 Lab — Build at the bench

DSGN 312 · Packaging Design · in the room

About 90 minutes across the sessions. Not graded — but observed.

The instructor is watching and will not help. A package the instructor folded has not been tested. Ask questions freely once your build is recorded; before that, expect questions back.

What you need

  • Your board — your specification, ordered for you. Three sheets
  • The product, the final dieline, the week 9 paper model
  • Fresh blades, mat, metal rule, bone folder, adhesive

Steps

1 · Check your dieline against the board that arrived. Caliper matches what you drew for? If not, fix the file before printing. Ten minutes here saves a sheet.

2 · Print at 100% on board. Verify with a rule.

3 · Cut, along cut lines only. Fresh blade, light passes.

4 · Crease with the bone folder against the rule, from the correct face for each fold. Mountain and valley behave differently.

5 · Fold and assemble in the order your structure requires — without instruction from anyone.

This is the part being observed. Not whether it is fast or neat: whether it assembles from your submitted dieline unaided.

6 · Put the product in. Close it.

7 · Run the handling test — all seven steps. Narrate what you notice.

8 · Record what failed and fix it in the file. Not on the model.

A repair made with tape is recorded as a repair. That is not a punishment — it is the only way the distinction survives, because a taped model photographs identically to a folded one.

9 · Second build from the corrected file. Most students need one and three sheets exist for that reason. A second build from a fixed file counts as success — the fix went into the drawing, which is the behaviour the course wants.

10 · Build the 3D visualization and put it beside the object. Check panel count, proportion, closure.

What is being recorded

Did the mockup assemble from the submitted dieline? Yes / no
Was tape, trimming or correction required to close it? What, and where
Did every glue tab land on a face that could receive it?
Did the product fit with the tolerance the caliper demanded?

None of this is recoverable from photographs. It is logged at the bench, per student, as it happens.

If it fails

Expected. Most first builds do.

Work the diagnosis in order — it is the same list as week 9, now in a material that shows:

Symptom Likely
Ragged, fibrous cut edge Dull blade. Change it
Box will not sit square Crease made without a tool, or from the wrong face
Crack through the artwork on a fold Creased from the wrong side, or against the grain
Tuck flap will not enter Caliper allowance too small
Tab has nothing to stick to Assembly order
Product will not fit Allowance too small — and it fitted in paper because paper is thin

Fix it in the file, print again, build again. That is the intended shape of the week.

Before you leave

  • A built mockup, in your specified material, holding your product
  • The handling test run, all seven steps
  • A 3D visualization that matches it
  • The object itself, kept safe. Week 11 cannot run without it
