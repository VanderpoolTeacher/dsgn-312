Week 10 Assignment — Phase 5, mockup and visualization

DSGN 312 · Packaging Design

Formative · satisfactory/unsatisfactory · required · observed. Phase 5 — completes Component C and feeds Component D.

Competencies 1 and 2, Competency Rubric.

What to hand in

  1. Photographs of the built mockup — assembled, with the product inside, and open
  2. The 3D visualization
  3. A side-by-side of the two, showing correspondence
  4. The handling test results — all seven steps, what happened at each
  5. What failed on the first build and what you changed in the file

The object itself is not submitted — it is observed at the bench and you keep it. Week 11 needs it.

You're done when

It closes, holds the product, opens without destroying itself, and survives handling — and it assembled from your submitted dieline.

Satisfactory

  • Built at full size in the specified material
  • All seven handling steps run and reported
  • Visualization corresponds — same panel count, proportion, closure
  • A first-build failure fixed in the file, if there was one

Not yet

  • Not built, or renders only
  • Taped into shape with no corrected file
  • A visualization that does not match the object
  • Handling test not run

Watch out for

Renders instead of an object. A render cannot fail to close, so it cannot evidence closing. This outcome cannot be met digitally and there is no version of it that can.

Tape as a solution. Tape is a diagnosis. The fix goes in the file, and the second build is what counts.

A visualization that improves the object. Cleaner proportions, a closure the box does not have, a finish the board cannot take. That is a different design, and it will contradict the object on the table in week 15.

Skipping the handling test because it "obviously works." Step 4 — opening it twice — is the one that catches most failures, and nobody does it unprompted.

Why the bench observation exists

A taped model photographs identically to a folded one.

Your photographs cannot settle whether the dieline was correct, and neither can your files. Only somebody watching at the moment of assembly can — which is why this is recorded in the room and cannot be reconstructed afterwards.

It is part of LO 2's evidence, and no later week reproduces the moment.

Submission

Photographs, visualization, and the report before the next session.

Bring the object to week 11. Week 11's summative cannot be attempted without it, and a single absence here costs both weeks.
