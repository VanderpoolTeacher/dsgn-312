Week 4 Lab — Materials sourcing

DSGN 312 · Packaging Design · in the room

About 50 minutes. Not graded. What you produce becomes this week's assignment, a module summative.

What you need

  • A sample product from the front. Not your own, and one with published material data so your claims can be checked
  • The sourcing worksheet
  • The swatch set, a caliper gauge, and the supplier reference sheets
  • A partner

Steps

1 · Take a supplied product. Using your own comes in week 7, when the attachment is the point. Today it would only distort the choice.

2 · Identify what it is made of now — as precisely as the packaging and the manufacturer's information allow. Some will tell you. Some will not, and "cannot determine from available information" is an honest answer.

3 · Specify a substrate yourself. Name, grammage or caliper, and whether it is actually available from a supplier. The reference sheets are on the table.

4 · Name the disposal stream that substrate enters.

5 · Name the disqualifying feature — the single thing in your construction most likely to knock it out of that stream. Every construction has one. If you cannot find yours, you have not looked at the finish, the adhesive, or the closure.

6 · Specify one alternative and reject it. What ruled it out — cost, stiffness at the span required, printability, or disposal?

7 · State the structural consequence. What does your caliper mean for a dieline drawn in week 3's terms?

8 · Swap worksheets with a partner.

Their only job: ask how do you know? of every number.

Not is it a good choice — where did the number come from? A figure nobody can source gets flagged.

9 · Fix what your partner flagged.

The partner check is the point of the lab

Students write "300 gsm" because it sounds right. It is the single most common thing wrong with a specification, and it is invisible to the person who wrote it.

A partner asking how do you know? finds it in seconds, and finds it more comfortably than an instructor does. Do it properly — a flagged number now is a mark kept later.

What a sourced number looks like

Unsourced Sourced
"300 gsm" "300 gsm — supplier sheet on the table, available in this size"
"It's recyclable" "Kerbside paper. The manufacturer's site states uncoated"
"About 0.4 mm" "0.38 mm — measured with the gauge"
"Compostable" "PLA — industrially compostable only. No facility locally"

Measuring it yourself counts as a source. That is what the gauge is for.

Before you leave

  • A substrate with a name and a number
  • A stream, and the feature that threatens it
  • One rejected alternative and what ruled it out
  • Every number either sourced or flagged by your partner
