DSGN 312 — Course Glossary

Every word this course uses in a particular way, and what it means here.

Some are ordinary words with a narrow meaning in packaging — a mockup is not a render, a crease is not a fold line, sustainable is not a claim until a stream is named. Some name something specific to this course. When an assignment or a rubric uses one of these words, this is the sense it means.

The Never use column is not a ban on your vocabulary. It records the near-synonyms that get used loosely, so that a word means in week 13 what it meant in week 3.

A module owns a term. It is introduced there, assessed there, and used freely everywhere after.

Two things worth knowing before you read it.

The six course outcomes are quoted exactly as the college wrote them. Where you see an outcome quoted, it is quoted.

The outcomes use ordinary packaging words, and this glossary cannot overrule them. Functional, brand identity, dielines, mockups, 3D visualizations, structure, hierarchy, material consideration, usability, sustainability, impact, critique, accessibility, portfolio, iterative design and technical execution all appear in them. None can be a barred word here — where one is loose, the entry sharpens it.

Part 1 · Course-wide

Term In this course Never use
Phase One of the eight steps of the final project, as set out in the student brief. Phases are what students do. Stage, part, milestone — the last means something else here
Component One of the seven things scored, A to G. Components are what is marked. A phase produces a component; they are not the same object. Deliverable on its own, which is ambiguous between the two
Container A component that carries no outcome and exists to make the others cohere. Component A is the only one. It is worth 10% and a student cannot pass on it. Filler, admin — it is real work, it just is not evidence
Formative Assessed for feedback, satisfactory/unsatisfactory. Required, not optional. Its purpose is to show you where you are while you can still act on it. Practice, ungraded — both imply it can be skipped
Module summative The assessment that asks whether a module's outcomes were reached. Nine weeks carry one. Test, exam — there are none in this course
Observed component Evidence that exists only if it is recorded as it happens: the week 10 assembly, the week 12 critique, the week 15 questioning. No file is submitted and none can be reconstructed afterwards. Participation — that is attendance, this is evidence
The account Your explanation of the work: what changed, why, and what is still wrong. Scored on its own rubric, separately from the work itself. Presentation when you mean the reasoning rather than the event
The work The designed and built object and its files. Scored on the summative rubric. —

Part 2 · Module vocabulary

Module 1 · Introduction — weeks 1–2

Term In this course Never use
Package The whole system: what holds the product, what it ships and sells in, and the surface design on both. Box — most packages are not boxes, and the habit narrows your thinking
Primary packaging What the product is actually in — the container, wrapper, or bottle. —
Secondary packaging What the primary sits in — the carton, sleeve, or carrier. Outer on its own
Friction A point where the package resisted the person opening it, located in the object and reproducible by somebody else. Annoying, hard to open — those are reactions, not findings
The covered-logo test Masking every occurrence of the mark and asking somebody unfamiliar what the package is, who it is for, and what it costs. Used in weeks 2 and 11. Blind test — that means something else in research

Module 2 · Structure and materials — weeks 3–4

Term In this course Never use
Dieline The flat drawing that instructs a machine where to cut and where to crease, at full size, with those instructions on separate named layers. Template — a template is something you fill in, a dieline is something you draw
Cut A line the blade goes through. —
Crease A line the material is compressed along so it folds cleanly. A crease is not a cut, and putting one on the wrong layer destroys the run. Fold line, score — the first is vague, the second means a partial cut
Glue tab A flap that adheres to another face. Where it can go is decided by assembly order, not by the drawing. Flap on its own
Substrate The material the package is made of, named specifically: a board type, a plastic, a compostable. Stock when you mean the material class
Caliper The thickness of the substrate. The dimension your dieline must allow for, and the reason a panel cut to the exact inside dimension will not close. Weight — that is a different measurement
Grammage The mass of the substrate, in gsm. Tells you stiffness, not thickness. Specifying grammage alone is not enough to draw with. Thickness, weight used loosely
Disposal stream The named route the package actually enters after use — kerbside paper, commercial composting, landfill. Recyclable as a substitute. That word names no route
Disqualifying feature The thing in your construction that would knock the package out of its stated stream: a lamination, an adhesive, a metallised film, a window. Naming it is worth more than naming the virtue. —

Module 3 · Storytelling and shelf — weeks 5–6

Term In this course Never use
First read What the eye takes in first, at the distance the package is actually seen from. It changes between two metres and arm's length, and the two are different surfaces. Focal point
Hierarchy The ranked order in which things are read, on a named panel at a named distance. A claim you can test by asking somebody to number it. Layout when you mean the reading order
Contrast A relationship, always against something specific. Matte in a row of gloss is contrast; bold is not. Pops, stands out — conclusions with the reasoning removed
Audience A description of a person specific enough that it rules something out and forces at least two design decisions. Target market, demographic — those are brackets, not audiences
The four lenses Market relevance, ethical considerations, accessibility, and visual communication. The four angles every critique in this course must cover, each finding something the others did not. Criteria — used for rubric rows
Critique Judgment with a consequence attached: what is wrong, and what would have to change. Feedback, review — both permit description without judgment

Module 4 · Concept and proposal — week 7

Term In this course Never use
Brief Component A. The brand, the product with its measured dimensions, the retail context, and the audience. The container everything else hangs on. Proposal, pitch
Specification Component B. Substrate, grammage or caliper, disposal stream, the rejected alternative and what ruled it out, and the structural consequence. Spec sheet — that is a printer's document
Retail context The named situation the package is bought in — a shelf in a co-op, a delivery box, a table at a market. Retail, online — categories, not situations

Module 5 · AI and ideation — weeks 8–9

Term In this course Never use
Prompt The exact text given to a generative tool, saved as it was written. Prompts are evidence and cannot be reconstructed afterwards. Query, input
Checkable failure A specific thing in a generated concept that could not be made, which somebody else can confirm from the image: a flap with no slot, a foil finish on compostable board, a claim that could not legally appear. Flaw, problem, it didn't understand
Attribution Deciding whether a failure was caused by an underspecified prompt or by the tool. The distinction decides what a designer does next. Blame
The paper check Printing a dieline at true 100% on cheap paper and folding it before cutting real material. Draft, test print

Module 6 · Prototyping — weeks 10–11

Term In this course Never use
Mockup The package built at full size in the specified material, assembled, holding the product. A physical object. Render, visual, comp — none of them can fail to close
3D visualization A digital image of the package that corresponds to the built object — same panel count, proportion and closure. A visualization that improves the object is a different design. Mockup, which is the physical thing
Assembled unaided Built from the submitted dieline without tape, trimming, or correction. Recorded at the bench in week 10, because a taped model photographs identically to a folded one. It worked
Test result Something a person who did not make the package did — where they hesitated, what they misread, what they could not open. Behaviour, not opinion. Feedback — opinions are cheap and mostly polite

Module 7 · Compliance and production — weeks 12–13

Term In this course Never use
Required information What a label must legally carry and where it must sit. It constrains layout absolutely and competes for the space the brand wants. Legal text as though it were optional
Accessibility failure A specific barrier to opening, reading, or handling: reversed 6 pt dosage text, a seal needing two hands, variants distinguished by colour alone. Not user-friendly
Destination The named process the files are prepared for — who would print this, on what, at what quantity. Every export setting answers to it, and without one the settings are guesses. Print-ready with no destination named
Production handoff Files a printer could run without phoning you. Tested by a peer opening them cold. Final files

Module 8 · Presentation and portfolio — weeks 14–15

Term In this course Never use
Dated state A saved, dated version of the design at a point in its history. Three are required: the selected concept, the post-test revision, the production version. Version, draft — neither implies the date, and the date is what makes it evidence
Cause What a revision responded to — a test result, a critique, a material constraint, a print limitation. A revision with no cause is a preference. Improvement, refinement
Case study Component G's document: problem and outcome first, then the route. Not chronological — a diary buries the finding. Process book, write-up

Part 3 · Terms retired

Retired Replaced by Why
Part 1–6 (Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test, Present) Phases 1–8 The pre-process summative used a six-part design-thinking frame. The current project has eight phases and seven scored components, and the old numbering no longer maps. The original is parked at assessments/_pre-process/
Eco-friendly Disposal stream + disqualifying feature Names no route and cannot be checked
Sustainable (used alone, as a claim) A stream, a percentage, or a rejected alternative The word survives in LO 3 and in the project title, where it describes an aim. As a claim about a specific package it is not evidence
User Audience, or the person who did the thing User blurs the buyer, the opener, and the tester, who are often three different people
Iteration (meaning: several versions) Dated state + cause Versions are not iteration. Iteration is change with a cause attached
