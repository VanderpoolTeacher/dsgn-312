DSGN 312 — Competency Rubric

The master rubric for every assignment in the course.

Six competencies, one per course learning outcome, four levels each. Every weekly assignment cites from here rather than inventing its own criteria.

Outcomes are reproduced verbatim from the course specification. Terms follow the Glossary.

How to use it

An assignment does not assess all six. Most assess one.

Each assignment states which competencies it evidences and what that week's specific evidence is. The level descriptors below do not change week to week — that is the point of a competency rubric. What changes is the artifact.

Level Means
Exceeds The competency is demonstrated, and the student can name what it cost or what it rules out
Meets The competency is demonstrated, with the evidence the artifact cannot carry
Approaching The artifact, without that evidence
Not yet Not demonstrated

Same four levels as the summative rubric and the presentation rubric, so a student meets one scheme across the whole course.

The line between Meets and Exceeds

Meets demonstrates it. Exceeds names what it cost.

Packaging is a discipline of trades — every choice buys one thing by giving up another, and a student who can only name the gain has not finished the reasoning. That sentence appears in this course under five names:

Where Called
Week 4, week 7 — materials The rejected alternative. What lost, and what ruled it out
Week 4, week 12 — sustainability The disqualifying feature. What would knock this out of the stream you named
Week 8 — AI Attribution. Did the prompt cause it, or the tool
Week 11, week 14 — revision The cause. What would have gone wrong had it stayed
Week 15 — the account The weakest point, volunteered before it is asked for

The near-miss this course is built to catch is the student who produces a correct artifact with the reasoning removed — a specification with one material, a file exported to a preset, three versions with no causes. All four Approaching rows below describe that same student.

Competency 1 — Packaging that communicates

LO 1. Design functional and visually engaging packaging solutions that communicate brand identity and appeal to specific audiences.

Level Descriptor
Exceeds The covered-logo test succeeds and the student names the audience decision that produced the result.
Meets Built at full size; it closes, holds the product, opens without destroying itself, survives handling. With the mark covered, somebody unfamiliar can say who it is for and roughly what it costs. Visual decisions trace to a named audience.
Approaching The object works, but with the mark covered the brand reads as generic — identity was carried by the logo alone. Or an audience is named and no decision traces to it.
Not yet Not built, or submitted as visualizations only. Fails to close, does not hold the product, or is destroyed by opening.

Competency 2 — Prototyping

LO 2. Develop dielines, mockups, and 3D visualizations to prototype packaging designs for print or digital presentation.

Level Descriptor
Exceeds Assembles first time from the submitted dieline, and the student can point to the allowance made for the substrate's own caliper.
Meets Three artifacts of one design. The mockup is cut from the submitted dieline and assembles without tape, trimming, or correction. Panel count, proportion and closure carry across all three.
Approaching The dieline is clean and correctly layered, but the mockup needed tape or trimming — a glue tab on the wrong face, or a panel short by its own caliper.
Not yet Fewer than three artifacts, or they describe different objects.

Competency 3 — Structure and material

LO 3. Apply principles of structure, hierarchy, and material consideration to ensure usability, sustainability, and impact.

Level Descriptor
Exceeds Names the disposal stream, its disqualifying feature, and a change made to the construction to protect it.
Meets Substrate, grammage or caliper, and disposal stream named. One alternative rejected with what ruled it out. The structural consequence stated. Reading order decided on the buyer-facing panel.
Approaching A material specified with real numbers, but nothing rejected — the choice is asserted rather than reasoned. Or a stream claimed with no disqualifying feature identified.
Not yet Material named by type only, with no grammage, no caliper, no route. A preference, not a specification.

Competency 4 — Critique

LO 4. Critique packaging systems based on market relevance, ethical considerations, accessibility, and visual communication.

Level Descriptor
Exceeds Finds a fault in one lens that changes the reading in another, and says which fix serves both.
Meets All four lenses, each finding something the others did not. Every fault named carries what would have to change. Delivered on work the student did not make.
Approaching Fluent and accurate, but one or more lenses go unaddressed, or faults arrive without consequences — a good description that never becomes a judgment.
Not yet Describes what is there without evaluating it, or addresses one lens only.

Competency 5 — Process and execution

LO 5. Produce a packaging portfolio piece that demonstrates concept development, iterative design, and technical execution.

Level Descriptor
Exceeds Names a revision that was made and then partly reversed, and says what the reversal taught.
Meets At least three dated states. Each revision names its cause and what would have gone wrong had it stayed. Files a printer could run without a phone call.
Approaching Three states shown and described accurately, but as a sequence of improvements rather than responses. Nothing caused anything.
Not yet States differ cosmetically, or the dates show them produced together once the design had settled.

Competency 6 — AI, critically used

LO 6. Use AI-assisted tools to generate or visualize packaging concepts, while critically evaluating their outputs.

Level Descriptor
Exceeds Distinguishes an output that missed because the prompt was underspecified from one that missed despite a precise prompt, and draws the right conclusion from each.
Meets Concepts kept with the prompts that produced them. At least one checkable failure named — one another person can confirm from the image — followed by what the student did instead.
Approaching Prompts and outputs complete, reflection present, but the evaluation is general: "it didn't understand structure." Nothing another person could check.
Not yet Outputs with no prompts, or "it helped me brainstorm," or blanket dismissal. Both extremes evaluate nothing.
