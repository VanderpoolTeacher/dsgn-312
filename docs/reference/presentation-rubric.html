The Packaging System — Presentation Rubric

Step 3c of the Course Build Process

Scored independently of the work presented. 15% of the project — component G.

Evidences: LO 5

Why this is separate

Making the work and accounting for the work are different competencies. Averaging them lets a polished presentation carry weak artifacts, and lets a strong maker who cannot explain their decisions pass an outcome they have not met. A student may score high here and low on the summative rubric, or the reverse — both are meaningful results, not errors.

What is scored here is the account: whether the design's history is real and whether the student holds the reasoning rather than reciting it. Whether the mockup closes is scored on the summative rubric, not here.

The iteration account

Level Descriptor
Exceeds Names a revision that was made and then partly reversed, and says what the reversal taught — the design's history includes a wrong turn, not just progress.
Meets At least three dated states shown. Each revision names the thing it responded to — a test result, a critique, a material constraint, a print limitation — and the student says what would have gone wrong had it not changed.
Approaching Three states are shown and the changes are described accurately, but as a sequence of improvements rather than responses. Nothing caused anything.
Not yet States differ cosmetically — a colourway, a type swap — or the dates show them produced together once the design was already settled. One design shown three times.

The material and sustainability defence

Level Descriptor
Exceeds Volunteers the weakest point in the sustainability claim before being asked, and says what it would take to close it.
Meets States why the chosen substrate beat the rejected one, names the disposal stream, and identifies what in the construction could disqualify it. The claim survives being pushed on.
Approaching The specification is recalled accurately but held as fact rather than reasoning — the student can say what was chosen and not why the alternative lost.
Not yet Falls back on the word sustainable without a stream, a threat, or a rejected alternative.

The AI account

Level Descriptor
Exceeds Distinguishes what the tool was genuinely useful for from what it appeared useful for, with an example of each.
Meets Names a specific failure in an AI output, what in the prompt did or did not cause it, and what was done instead.
Approaching Describes the AI's contribution accurately but evaluates it only in general terms — helpful, limited, inconsistent — with no single checkable failure to point at.
Not yet "It helped me brainstorm," or a blanket claim that the outputs were useless.

Production handoff

Level Descriptor
Exceeds Names a setting that had to change for this substrate specifically, and why the default would have failed on it.
Meets Shows the production files and says which requirement each setting satisfies — dimensions with bleed carried through the folds, cut-and-crease on its own layer, resolution and colour space matched to the stated destination. A printer could run them without a phone call.
Approaching The files are correct, but the settings are reported rather than explained — "exported as PDF/X-1a" rather than what that answers.
Not yet Cannot show the files, or the settings do not match the stated destination.

Note on this criterion. It is the one row here that inspects an artifact, because Step 2b assigned production readiness to component G and splitting G's weight would contradict the summative's weighting table. It is written to score the account of the settings — the file is the prompt for the question, not the object of the mark. Flagged for review when the package is next revised.

Questions

Roughly five minutes of questioning after each presentation, from the instructor and from the room, with the physical mockups on the table and in people's hands.

Questions are assessment, not formality. They are what separates reasoning the student holds from reasoning they have rehearsed — a prepared deck can carry a recited account all the way to the end, and only an unscripted question finds out whether the thinking is there.

Expect at minimum:

  • Why this material and not the one you rejected?
  • What did the AI get wrong?
  • What would you change with another two weeks — and what would you leave alone?

A student who answers "I'd polish it" has not identified a weakness. A student who names the thing they know is still wrong, and why they ran out of room to fix it, has.
