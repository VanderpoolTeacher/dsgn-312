Week 7 Assignment — Brief and specification

DSGN 312 · Packaging Design

Module summative. Phase 1 and Phase 2 of the final project — these become Components A and B.

Competencies 1 and 3, Competency Rubric.

What to hand in

Two documents.

Phase 1 — The brief (Component A)

  1. The product, with three measured dimensions
  2. The brand — three sentences on what it stands for
  3. The retail context — a named situation, not a category
  4. The audience — one sentence that rules something out
  5. The two design decisions it forces

One page.

Phase 2 — The specification (Component B)

  1. Substrate, with grammage or caliper
  2. Disposal stream, with conditions
  3. The disqualifying feature
  4. One rejected alternative and what ruled it out
  5. The structural consequence for the dieline

One to two pages.

You're done when

Your audience rules something out, and something was rejected on materials.

Both tests are binary. Either a decision is forced or it is not. Either an alternative lost or none was considered.

Meets

  • Product measured in three axes
  • Audience statement rules something out, with two decisions named
  • Substrate named with grammage or caliper, stream named, disqualifying feature identified
  • One real alternative rejected, with what ruled it out

Exceeds

The retail context and audience are specific enough that two design decisions are already forced by them and you say which — and on materials, you name the stream, the threat, and a change you made to protect it.

Not yet

  • No measured dimensions. This fails here rather than in week 9, which is the point of assessing it now
  • A demographic bracket for an audience
  • A material named by type with no number, no route
  • A straw-man rejection

Watch out for

Keeping your options open. The strongest instinct this week and the most expensive. An audience that forces nothing gives you no reason to prefer any design in week 9, and you will feel it as an inability to decide.

Protecting the material you want. In week 4 you had no stake. Now you do, and the temptation is to reject something obviously worse so your preference survives untested. Reject a real alternative.

Transcription errors. Measure twice. A dimension written down wrong propagates into week 9's drawing and is discovered in week 10, in material, when it is expensive.

Over-scope. More pieces is not more ambition, it is less finished. Primary and secondary.

Starting the surface design. Week 8 ideates; week 9 puts artwork onto a structure that exists. Work done now gets defended later against the dieline that should have shaped it.

Why this is the hinge

Almost everything downstream depends on these two documents. Week 9 draws to your dimensions. Week 10 cuts your substrate. Week 13 produces against your specification.

They are also Components A and B — revised, not replaced. The standard applied here is the standard those components carry.

Submission

Two documents before the next session.

Keep the product safe. You need it in week 9 and again in week 10.
