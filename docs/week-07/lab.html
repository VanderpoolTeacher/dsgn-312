Week 7 Lab — Proposals and the specification clinic

DSGN 312 · Packaging Design · in the room

About 70 minutes across the sessions. Not graded — but everything in it becomes this week's assignment, which is a module summative.

What you need

  • Your product. Physically
  • The measurement and specification sheet
  • Callipers or an accurate rule
  • The swatch set and supplier reference sheets

Part one — proposals round the room

1 · Ninety seconds each. The product, its three dimensions, the brand, where it is bought, and who picks it up.

2 · The room's only job is to attack your audience statement. Name something it fails to rule out. Every statement gets at least one challenge — including the good ones, because a statement that survives a challenge is stronger for it.

3 · Rewrite it live. In the room, out loud, until it forces two decisions.

4 · Write the two decisions next to it. Not the persona — the decisions. Interior must look finished. Closure must be re-sealable.

Part two — the specification clinic

5 · Measure properly. Three axes, callipers, written down. Measure twice. A number transcribed wrong today propagates silently into week 9 and is not discovered until week 10, in material.

6 · Specify: substrate name, grammage or caliper, disposal stream, the disqualifying feature.

7 · Reject one alternative and say what ruled it out.

8 · The partner check, from week 4. Swap. Their only job: ask how do you know? of every number. Anything unsourced is flagged.

9 · Scope check with the instructor. Anything beyond primary plus secondary gets negotiated down before you leave.

If your product fails measurement

Take a spare from the front and restart at step 1. Today.

This is normal, it happens every year, and it is the reason spares exist. A student who insists their irregular product will be fine is a student with a blocked week 9.

What "forces a decision" looks like

Statement Forces
"Someone buying a gift they won't see opened" Finished interior · re-sealable closure · discreet price
"Someone standing up outdoors, holding a bag" One-handed opening · no flat surface needed · grip
"Someone comparing two of these on a shelf at eye level" The differentiator must survive at two metres
"Eco-conscious millennials" Nothing

Before you leave

  • Three measured dimensions, written, checked twice
  • An audience statement that survived the room, and two decisions written under it
  • A substrate with a name and a number, every number sourced
  • One rejected alternative and what ruled it out
  • A scope the instructor has signed off
