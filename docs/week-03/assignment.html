Week 3 Assignment — The folded dieline

DSGN 312 · Packaging Design

Formative · satisfactory/unsatisfactory · required. Competency 2, Competency Rubric.

What to hand in

  1. Your dieline file — layers named CUT and CREASE
  2. A photograph of the folded box, however it turned out
  3. The first failure, in a short paragraph: what you observed, where it is in the drawing, and which of the four causes it was
  4. What you changed on the second attempt, and whether it worked

Half a page plus the photograph and the file.

You're done when

The failure is diagnosed in the drawing.

A glue tab on the wrong face and a panel short by its own thickness are different faults with different fixes. Neither is a printing error — and if you verified the scale in step 4 of the lab, you have already ruled that out.

Satisfactory

  • The first failure is named, not the whole list of symptoms
  • It is located in the drawing — a panel, a tab, a flap
  • The second change was one change, and you say whether it worked

Not yet

  • "It didn't work"
  • "The printer scaled it" after verifying the scale
  • Three symptoms of one fault presented as three faults
  • No file, or layers not separated

Watch out for

Repairing before recording. The instinct is to fix it and then describe the fixed version. The first failure is the assignment, and it disappears the moment you tape something.

Diagnosing a consequence. If the tab is on the wrong face, the box will also not sit square, the lid will not align, and the whole thing will bow. Those are three consequences of one fault.

Blaming the material. Card that tears is usually a dull blade. That is a real finding and it belongs in the paragraph — but it is a cutting fault, not a drawing fault, and say which you have.

Changing three things at once. Then you do not know what fixed it, and neither does anybody reading it.

If your box closed first time

Say so, and say what you got right — specifically. Which of the four causes did you avoid, and how did you avoid it?

Then check it properly: product in, closed, opened, set down, picked up. A box that closes and will not reopen has failed at something week 1 taught you to look for.

Why this is required

Week 9 draws a real dieline to a measured product. Week 10 cuts it in real board. Both assume you have already had a box refuse to close and worked out why.

The failure is free this week. In week 10 it costs a sheet of specified material and a session.

Submission

File, photograph, and the paragraph, before the next session.
