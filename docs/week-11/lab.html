Week 11 Lab — Testing on strangers

DSGN 312 · Packaging Design · in the room and just outside it

About 60 minutes. Not graded — produces the module summative.

What you need

  • The built mockup, product inside
  • The observation sheet
  • Your sealed predictions
  • Masking material
  • Strangers — arranged, and briefed not to be helpful

Steps

1 · Hand it over and say nothing.

Not "it opens like this." Not "it's for X." Nothing. If you explain, the test is void — go to a fresh stranger and count the lost one as a lesson.

2 · Watch for thirty seconds and record behaviour. Hands, hesitation, what they turned over, what they tried first. Do not interpret yet — write what happened.

3 · Three questions, in order. What is it? Who is it for? What would it cost?

4 · Record verbatim. Exact words.

5 · Cover the mark. Second stranger. Same three questions.

6 · Compare against your sealed predictions, in writing, before you come back.

7 · Second tester, cold — required if the first one agreed with you.

One person's confusion might be that person. Two makes it the package. This is the step that gets skipped and it is what separates a finding from an anecdote.

8 · Peer critique in pairs, four lenses, on each other's built objects. Same instrument as week 6 — and as next week.

9 · Write the revision list. Each item traced to a specific observed behaviour. Mark which land in the dieline, which in the artwork, and which you are refusing and why.

10 · Save a dated copy of your file. Then make at least one revision.

The dated pre-revision state is week 14's middle state. Overwriting it is unrecoverable.

Briefing your stranger

Say only this:

"I'd like you to open this. I'm not going to explain it. If you get stuck, stop and tell me what you expected instead."

People are kind. They will work hard to open something and then say it was fine. The "stop when stuck" instruction is what gets you the real answer.

What a recorded behaviour looks like

❌ "They found it a bit confusing."

✅ "Turned it over twice. Tried the base first. Found the tuck flap after about eight seconds, then used two hands to open it."

Times, counts, sequence. Somebody else reading your notes should be able to picture it.

If the covered-logo test comes back blank

The stranger says "no idea who it's for."

This is the test working. Your week 7 audience statement did not reach the design — and you have four weeks, which is enough. Record it plainly, work out which decision should have carried the audience and did not, and put that on the revision list.

Do not hide it. It is a better finding than a comfortable one, and week 15's rubric rewards volunteering the weakness in your own work.

Before you leave

  • Behaviour recorded from at least two strangers
  • Verbatim answers to all three questions, twice
  • Predictions compared, in writing
  • A revision list, each item traced to a behaviour
  • A dated pre-revision file, saved
  • One revision made
