Week 9 Assignment — Phase 4, dieline and flat artwork

DSGN 312 · Packaging Design

Module summative. Phase 4 of the final project — the first half of Component C.

Competency 2, Competency Rubric.

What to hand in

  1. The dieline, native file plus export, layers named CUT · CREASE · ARTWORK · DIELINE-REF
  2. The flat artwork, positioned on the dieline, bleed running past every fold
  3. A dimensions table — every panel, and what it traces back to
  4. The caliper allowance you used, and where you applied it
  5. A photograph of the assembled paper version with the product inside

You're done when

The panels add up, and every dimension traces back to the product you measured in week 7.

Both are arithmetic, and both are checkable against objects that will be in the room — your specification and your product.

Meets

  • Layers correctly separated and named
  • Caliper allowance applied, and you can say where
  • Paper version assembles and holds the product
  • Bleed runs past every fold
  • Every dimension traceable

Exceeds

The paper version assembled first time, and you can point to the allowance you made for the board's own caliper and say why it is that number rather than a rule of thumb.

Not yet

  • Panels that do not add up
  • Dimensions that do not match week 7 and no explanation
  • A crease on the cut layer
  • Artwork stopping at folds

Watch out for

Dimension drift. Students adjust numbers to make a drawing tidy. The product is the reference, not the drawing. If they disagree, measure again.

The tuck flap cut to exact depth. The commonest failure in this week's work. It looks right and cannot enter.

Assuming one print round. The paper check exists to be failed. Two rounds is normal; a first-time success is worth checking rather than celebrating.

Bleed stopping at the crease. Compare against demo piece A on the bench. If you see a white line on your paper fold, you have it.

Leaving DIELINE-REF visible in the export. Your notes will print.

Why this is a summative

Week 10 cuts this drawing in real board, ordered per student against the week 7 specification. A dieline that fails in board costs a sheet and a session, and week 10 has no slack — week 11 tests what week 10 builds.

The paper check is what makes that survivable. This assignment is the evidence it happened.

Submission

Files, table, and photograph before the next session.

Bring the product and your board to week 10.
