Week 9 Lab — The paper check

DSGN 312 · Packaging Design · in the room

About 60 minutes. Not graded. Produces Phase 4, a module summative.

What you need

  • Your dieline file, your product, your week 7 specification
  • Cheap paper — five sheets
  • Callipers, rule, bone folder, knife, mat
  • A partner

Not board. Board is week 10.

Steps

1 · Print the dieline at true 100%. Verify against a known dimension with a rule. Ten seconds — same check as weeks 3 and 9, because it is repeatedly the invisible failure.

2 · Cut and fold it in paper. This is a geometry test, not a material test — paper is faster, cheaper, and tells you everything about whether the panels add up.

3 · Put the actual product inside it.

The whole point of the exercise. Does it fit, with the tolerance your caliper demands? A box that closes empty and will not close full has failed.

4 · Assemble it fully, including every tab. Find out whether each one has a face to land on.

5 · Record what failed, and fix it in the file. Not on the paper. A repair made with tape is a repair you will make again in board.

6 · Reprint and refold.

Expect two rounds. Budget three. Five sheets are provided for that reason. A room that reprints twice is a room that builds successfully next week.

7 · Partner check — hand your paper version to a partner and have them assemble it without instruction.

If they cannot work out the order, week 10's build will lose time to the same confusion — and in week 10 the instructor is watching and recording.

8 · Check bleed at every fold, on the flat artwork, against the folded paper model and against demo piece A on the bench.

Diagnosing, in order

Symptom Likely
Product does not fit Caliper allowance too small
Tuck flap will not enter Flap cut to the exact internal depth — add ~2 caliper
Tab has nothing to stick to Assembly order — the face is covered by then
Box will not sit square Crease placement, or a panel out by a millimetre
White line along a fold Artwork stopped at the crease
Everything is slightly wrong Print scale. Check it first

Nobody cuts board today

The instinct to jump to the real material is strongest among the students most likely to get it nearly right.

Board is week 10, after the geometry is proven. A student who cuts early and gets it wrong has burned specified material — which was ordered per student — and a week.

Before you leave

  • A paper version that assembles, holds the product, and closes
  • Every dimension traceable to week 7's measurements
  • A partner who assembled yours unaided
  • Bleed running past every fold
